Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Search Misaligns Instruction-Tuning

Agentic reinforcement learning helps language models use search, but its effect on safety learned through instruction tuning is not well understood. Across Qwen and Llama models, the authors find that models often redirect harmful requests to benign searches when they can refuse first. A minimal trigger that calls search before refusal exposes a broader weakness, with RL-trained models producing multi-step harmful searches. The analysis links this behaviour to a direction in the residual stream associated with search-query safety. Penalising movement towards the harmful end of this direction during RL training restores instruction-tuning-level safety while preserving question-answering accuracy.

Key result

Under a minimal search trigger, search-query safety fell by up to 68.6% in Qwen and 69.6% in Llama models compared with their instruction-tuned counterparts. A reward penalty on the harmful search direction restored instruction-tuned safety without reducing benign question-answering accuracy.
