Can sparse autoencoders be used to decompose and interpret steering vectors?

Steering vectors can alter a language model’s behaviour, but explaining what they represent remains difficult. Sparse autoencoders appear to offer interpretable features, yet reconstructed steering vectors often lose the effect of the originals. This paper examines why direct decomposition is misleading. Steering vectors can fall outside the distribution on which the autoencoder was trained, and negative projections onto feature directions can carry information that standard decompositions miss. Both problems limit what a feature list alone can reveal about steering.

Key result

Direct sparse autoencoder decompositions can misrepresent steering vectors because of distribution shift and meaningful negative feature projections.
