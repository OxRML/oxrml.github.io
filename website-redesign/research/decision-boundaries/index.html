LLMs Don't Know Their Own Decision Boundaries: The Unreliability of Self-Generated Counterfactual Explanations

Counterfactual explanations ask what small change would alter a model’s decision. The researchers found that language models can often propose a change that flips their own answer, but the changes are frequently larger than necessary. When prompted to make them minimal, models often produce edits that do not flip the answer at all, limiting their value as explanations of a model’s decision boundary.

Key result

Models often found answer-changing edits, but struggled to make them both valid and minimal.
