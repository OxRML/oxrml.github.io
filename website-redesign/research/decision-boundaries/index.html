LLMs Don't Know Their Own Decision Boundaries: The Unreliability of Self-Generated Counterfactual Explanations

A counterfactual explanation changes an input so that a model would give a different answer. To explain a decision well, that change must actually flip the answer and be no larger than needed. This study tests whether language models can produce such explanations of their own predictions across several models, datasets and evaluation settings. Models usually found edits that changed their answers, but those edits were far from minimal. Requests for smaller edits often produced changes that failed to alter the prediction.

Key result

Self-generated counterfactual explanations showed a persistent trade-off between changing a model’s answer and keeping the edit minimal.
