Evaluating Fine-Tuning Efficiency of Human-Inspired Learning Strategies in Medical Question Answering

Fine-tuning medical question answering models is expensive, so the order of training examples could matter. This study tests five strategies inspired by human learning across four models, three datasets and data labelled by people or language models. The best gain was 1.81 per cent and the average gain was 1.02 per cent. Interleaving performed best on average, but the strongest ordering changed by model and dataset. Model-assigned difficulty labels also outperformed human labels in curriculum learning.

Key result

Human-inspired data ordering brought an average accuracy gain of 1.02 per cent, with interleaving strongest on average but no universal winner.
